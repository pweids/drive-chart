Drive Chart

Reading a play

Guide
Before the snap
  • Personnel. Count running backs and tight ends. "11" means 1 RB, 1 TE, so 3 WRs. "12", "21" and "13" are heavier groupings that lean toward runs and play-action.
  • QB alignment. Under center leans run or play-action. Shotgun leans pass, though many teams run plenty from it.
  • Safeties. One deep in the middle ("single-high") usually means Cover 1 (man) or Cover 3. Two deep ("two-high") usually means Cover 2 or Cover 4. Two-high leaves fewer defenders near the line, which invites runs.
  • Box count. Count defenders within about 5 yards of the ball. If they outnumber the blockers, the run is hard.
  • Motion. If a defender runs across the field with the motion man, the defense is probably in man coverage. If the defenders just shift over, it is probably zone.
At the snap
  • Watch the offensive line for the first second, not the ball. Linemen firing forward means run. Linemen stepping back means pass. Linemen stepping back while the QB fakes a handoff means play-action.
  • Run scheme. If all five linemen step the same direction in unison, it is a zone run. If a guard pulls across behind the line, it is a gap or power run, and the back follows the puller.
  • Rushers. Count them. Five or more is a blitz, which usually means man coverage behind it and a quicker throw.
After the play
  • Where did the ball end up relative to the sticks? On 3rd down, a completion short of the line to gain is still a failed down.
  • Who won the rep? On a sack, find the blocker who got beat. On a long run, find the hole and the block that opened it.
  • On replays, broadcasts often show the end-zone or high wide angle. Use it to check the safeties and the line, since the live camera follows the ball.
The numbers here
  • Success rate. A play succeeds if it gains 40% of the needed yards on 1st down, 60% on 2nd, and all of them on 3rd or 4th. One 60-yard run can inflate yards per play; success rate counts each down equally.
  • Dropbacks are pass attempts, sacks, and QB scrambles. ESPN logs scrambles as runs, so this page reclassifies them as pass plays.
  • Kneel-downs, spikes, kickoffs, punts and kicks are left out of run/pass counts.
